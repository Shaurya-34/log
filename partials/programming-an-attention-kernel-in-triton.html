The wall, named honestly

Here's what this kernel is not: it is not FlashAttention, and it's worth being precise about why, rather than letting the name imply more than the code does.

Step 01

All of K and V, in one shot

This kernel loads the entire K and V into fast on-chip memory (SRAMThe GPU's small, extremely fast on-chip memory, as opposed to its much larger but far slower off-chip global memory.) in one shot before doing anything else.

Step 02

SRAM runs out

That's fine at the sequence lengths tested here, but SRAM is small (tens of kilobytes per streaming multiprocessor, not gigabytes), and K/V grow linearly with sequence length. At some sequence length, "all of K and V" simply stops fitting, and this kernel breaks, not gracefully, it just runs out of room.

Step 03

Chunks, and a running max and sum

The real FlashAttention trick is to never need all of K/V in SRAM at once: process it in chunks, and keep a running max and running sum as you go, correcting the accumulated output every time a new chunk reveals a bigger max than anything seen so far.

Step 04

The rung not yet climbed

That's a genuinely different, harder algorithm than anything in this post, the online-softmax rescaling has no analogue in kernels 1 through 4, and it's the specific thing I haven't built yet.
